Policies We Hold

The following policies are maintained, reviewed regularly, and available in full to parents, carers, referring schools, colleges and local authorities on request:

  • Safeguarding & Child Protection Policy — aligned to Keeping Children Safe in Education (KCSIE) 2026 and Working Together to Safeguard Children (2026)
  • Safer Recruitment & DBS Policy — Enhanced DBS checks for all staff and volunteers, safer recruitment practice at every stage of hiring
  • Health & Safety Policy — covering all provision settings including outdoor/Forest School and Animal Assisted Learning environments
  • Risk Assessment — premises and activity-specific hazard and control record, reviewed annually
  • Data Protection Policy — compliant with UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018
  • Behaviour Management Policy — a relational, trauma-informed approach to behaviour
  • Complaints Policy — a clear, fair route for parents, carers, schools and local authorities to raise concerns
  • Equality, Diversity & Accessibility Policy — aligned to the Equality Act 2010 and SEND Code of Practice 2015
  • Online Safety & Acceptable Use Policy — covering device use, Digital Detox sessions and staff online conduct
  • Educational Visits & Off-Site Activities Policy — covering Forest School and community-based Life Skills sessions
  • Animal Welfare Policy — aligned to the Animal Welfare Act 2006, for our Animal Assisted Learning provision
  • Staff Code of Conduct — professional standards and boundaries, including low-level concerns reporting
  • Whistleblowing Policy — a safe route for staff to raise concerns about practice
  • Anti-Bullying Policy — covering peer-on-peer behaviour and cyberbullying
  • Attendance & Registration Policy — accurate recording and reporting to referring schools and local authorities
  • Supporting Young People with Medical Conditions Policy — including the administration of medicines
  • Lone Working Policy — safeguards for staff working 1:1 or off-site

Our Operating Model

L-Fit Pathways operates as an unregistered alternative provision, delivering fewer than 18 hours of provision per week to each young person, in line with Department for Education thresholds for unregistered settings. Above this threshold, a setting is required to register as an independent school with Ofsted — we structure our provision to operate transparently within the unregistered model, and welcome scrutiny from commissioning schools and local authorities on this at any time.

We also monitor the Department for Education’s developing National Standards for Alternative Provision — currently being introduced on a voluntary basis ahead of expected future legislation — and are committed to meeting them as they are formalised.

Statutory & Legislative Framework

Our practice is governed by the following legislation and statutory guidance:

Keeping Children Safe in Education (KCSIE) 2026

DfE statutory safeguarding guidance for education settings, effective from 1 September 2026.

Working Together to Safeguard Children (2026)

Statutory guidance on inter-agency working to safeguard and promote the welfare of children, published March 2026.

Children Act 1989 & Children Act 2004

The core legal framework for child welfare and the duty to safeguard and promote the welfare of children.

Education Act 1996, Section 19

The local authority duty to arrange suitable full-time education for children who cannot attend school (the basis of EOTAS placements).

Arranging Alternative Provision — DfE Guidance for Local Authorities & Schools

Statutory guidance on commissioning and quality-assuring alternative provision.

SEND Code of Practice 2015 (0–25 years)

Statutory guidance on supporting children and young people with special educational needs and disabilities.

Equality Act 2010

Ensuring provision is accessible and free from discrimination for young people with protected characteristics, including disability.

UK GDPR & Data Protection Act 2018

Governing how personal and sensitive data on young people and families is collected, stored and shared.

Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974

The legal foundation for risk assessment and safe delivery across all our provision settings.

The Prevent Duty (Counter-Terrorism and Security Act 2015)

The duty to have due regard to preventing people being drawn into terrorism.
